# Product experience before implementation proof

The promotional outline now establishes what users can broadly do before using a metric to validate one capability. This extends the existing thesis and audience-value work without weakening source ownership or factual review.

## Findings and changes

1. **Why 307/309 appeared too early.** The previous classification put both capabilities and validation under `CORE_PROOF`. Audience-weighted ranking and the preferred numeric-proof rule made PSD evidence attractive; preserving source order put the PSD section immediately after identity. The first core body visit was then labeled `core-experience` by position, even though it contained a test result. The broad feature quote remained bundled into identity evidence and could be cited without its breadth being spoken. The saved baseline scored thesis fidelity and audience value 5/5 without any breadth dimension.

2. **Core experience classification.** `CORE_EXPERIENCE` describes user capabilities and workflow. Each claim also carries `experienceScope` (`broad`, `focused`, `none`) and up to eight literal `experienceFacets`. Model facets must occur in their assigned quote. The conservative fallback separates grouped capability prose from test/benchmark evidence. Literal overview sentences can now be recovered independently even when a longer identity claim contains them.

3. **Proof treatment.** `CORE_PROOF` now describes implementation evidence. Meaningful numeric capability proof remains available and preferred, but its score cannot displace the reserved broad-experience slot. One proof and one useful deeper capability compete for the remaining body runtime. Technical validation and micro-caveats still face the existing audience-value filter; developer and tutorial modes retain their source evidence.

4. **Beat ordering and ranking.** Promotional selection reserves identity and available broad experience before filling proof/depth slots. A colocated overview becomes a separate visit on the same mapped section, preserving camera continuity and local citations. Roles come from classification rather than assigning “experience” to the first proof. The writer receives the ordered roles and must retain the reserved broad visit. The normal shape is identity → experience → proof → useful depth → caveat/takeaway. Source order remains mandatory: earlier proof/depth is omitted if the available experience context occurs later. Unsupported stages are not manufactured.

5. **Breadth evaluation.** `productBreadth` is scored independently of thesis fidelity and audience value. Scores ≤3 require revision and cannot be outweighed by polished style. Deterministic checks compare spoken sentences with distinct source-backed capability facets; merely citing a broad quote earns no coverage. `product-breadth` flags underselling and `proof-before-experience` flags evidence without context. Several grouped capabilities suffice; narrating the whole catalogue is unnecessary.

6. **Endings.** The model critic evaluates whole-product synthesis, excessive narrow-feature repetition and repetition across opening/overview/proof/ending. `repetitive-takeaway` also checks whether a final recap repeats distinctive terms from already narrated proof rather than the overall product. Grounding rules still require explicit, previously narrated same-source takeaway citations.
